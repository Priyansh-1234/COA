Question 1: 4-bit Sequential Booth Multiplier with Visible Iterations
1. Objective
Design and implement a 4-bit signed sequential multiplier using Booth’s Multiplication
Algorithm.
Both operands are 4-bit signed two’s-complement numbers, and the final product is an 8-bit
signed two’s-complement number.
The multiplication must be carried out iteratively. One Booth iteration must be performed
on every rising edge of a slow clock generated from the FPGA board clock. The slow-clock
technique demonstrated in the laboratory may be used so that the progress of the multiplication
can be observed on the board LEDs.
A purely combinational implementation using the Verilog multiplication operator is not permitted.

7. Slow Clock and Visible Execution
The FPGA board clock is too fast for the individual Booth iterations to be observed directly.
Use the slow-clock generation technique demonstrated in the laboratory. A counter may be
incremented using the board clock, and a suitable higher-order counter bit may be used as
slow_clk.
For example,
Board Clock −→ Clock-Divider Counter −→ slow_clk
may be used.
One complete Booth iteration must occur on each rising edge of slow_clk.
The selected clock division must be slow enough for the four successive iterations to be visibly
distinguished during the FPGA-board demonstration.
Students may temporarily connect the slow-clock signal to an LED to verify visible LED
blinking before integrating the Booth multiplier.

8. Formation of the Final Product
After four iterations, the final signed 8-bit product must be taken from
product[7:0] = {A[3 : 0], Q[3 : 0]}
where A[4] is the sign-extension bit of the internal accumulator.
Once the fourth iteration is complete:
• a done condition must be asserted internally;
• the product must remain stable; and
• no further Booth iterations must be performed until the circuit is reset and started again.

Write a behavioral testbench for the multiplier.
The testbench must verify:
• reset and initialization;

• correct Booth decision for 01, 10, 00, and 11;

13. Design Restrictions
The following requirements must be satisfied:
1. The Verilog multiplication operator * must not be used to compute the product.
2. The design must be sequential and iterative. The complete multiplication must not be
performed in a single clock cycle.
3. Exactly one Booth iteration must be performed per slow-clock edge.
6

4. Sequential registers must be updated using non-blocking assignments.
5. The shift of {A, Q, Q−1 } must be an arithmetic right shift, preserving the sign of A.
6. The final product must remain unchanged after completion until the design is reset for
another multiplication.

14. Mandatory FPGA-Board Demonstration
Demonstration on the FPGA board is mandatory.
Each group must demonstrate the complete Booth multiplier to a Teaching Assistant.
The demonstration must include:
1. Setting signed multiplicand and multiplier values using the board switches.
2. Resetting and starting the multiplier.
3. Showing the internal Booth state changing on the LEDs for each slow-clock iteration.
4. Showing that the iteration count progresses through the four Booth steps.
5. Switching to the final-result display and showing the correct signed 8-bit product.
6. Demonstrating at least one positive-result case and one negative-result case.
The TA may request different operand values during the live demonstration.
