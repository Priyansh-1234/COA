Question 1: Bidirectional Circular LED Shift Register
Design and implement a 5-bit bidirectional circular shift register whose output is displayed
using five LEDs on the FPGA board.
The circuit must operate using a slow clock derived from the on-board clock. The slow-clock
generation method demonstrated in the laboratory using a counter and one of its higher-order
bits may be used.

2. Slow Clock Generation
The FPGA board clock is too fast for the LED changes to be observed directly.
Use the clock-divider technique demonstrated in the laboratory.
A sufficiently wide counter may be incremented on every rising edge of the board clock:

clk −→ Clock-Divider Counter −→ slow_clk
A higher-order bit of the counter may be used as the slow clock. The counter width and
selected bit should be chosen such that the LED movement is clearly visible to a human observer.
For example, the demonstrated approach used a counter of the form
reg [25:0] clk_div
and a higher-order bit of this counter as the slow clock.
Students may use the same idea or select another suitable higher-order bit depending on the
FPGA board clock frequency.
Slow-Clock Board Demonstration

12. Verilog Design Requirements
The design must use sequential Verilog constructs appropriately.
Students must use:
• edge-triggered always blocks for sequential logic;
• non-blocking assignments (<=) for register updates;
• a register/counter for slow-clock generation; and
• a 5-bit register for storing the LED pattern.
The LED pattern must be generated by the sequential shift-register operation and must not
be implemented as a combinational lookup table.
